## 6. Design decisions

- **Decision:** Two files, RULES.md (root, absolute) and PREFERENCES.md (user, defaults).
  **Why:** Half the old ruleset was load-bearing for the scripts and half was house style.
  People copying XAW should be able to drop P-09 without touching what makes pickup work.
  **Alternatives rejected:** One file with per-rule tags; a separate overrides file.
- **Decision:** Old R-IDs were renumbered despite the old "never renumber" rule.
  **Why:** Human instruction (quoted in section 2). A clean R-01…R-15 / P-01…P-10 split is
  worth one break; the old IDs survive only in immutable historical entries.
  **Alternatives rejected:** Keeping gaps in the R sequence.
- **Decision:** check.sh skips handoffs/handoffs/ when validating citations.
  **Why:** Entries are immutable (P-02) and legitimately cite the IDs of their time.
- **Decision:** Preferences change at setup (procedure step 5) or on a quoted human
  instruction, never silently. Rules never change at all (R-15).
- **Decision (old R-18, now P-05 + R-15):** RULES.md edited on the human instruction in
  section 2.
- **Decision (old R-07):** "Exactly one entry per session" became "every session ends with a
  handoff" (R-06). The count was house style; the existence is the mechanism.

## 7. Philosophical choices

- Separate what the machine needs from what the house wants. Root rules protect invariants;
  preferences express taste and are expected to differ between projects.
- A renumbering is acceptable once, at a structural split, if history is left alone.
